This is an AI methods/source review of the revised proposal, active plan and research instructions. It is not human architectural sign-off, a new benchmark result, a full systematic literature review or permission to construct/execute the proposed study. Existing literature ledgers, construction, protocols, raw results and allocation records remain unchanged.

## Verdict

The direction is substantially better grounded than the original F# token-economics claim. It asks about **credible architectural packages and later behavioral obligations**, with Nu provisional and no requirement for a favorable F# result. It can justify a small feasibility investigation. It does not yet establish an executable case, a generally identified architectural mechanism or guaranteed publication value.

The strongest contribution is not “architecture matters” or “no one compared these Nu examples.” It is to make source-grounded predictions about which future changes expose different organizational trade-offs, then test those predictions with obligation-level evidence over inherited states. The smallest credible next decision is whether to authorize A0, not all 18 reference checkpoints or 192 candidate requests.

## Findings and corrections

### R1 — Recover a close predecessor in the current synthesis

The code-cleanliness minimal-pair study already manipulates code before controlled downstream tasks; it is not merely evidence about formatting. Its primary methods include refactoring/degradation, hotspot and multi-module tasks, and insensitive controls. It was discussed in older ALF work but absent from the latest proposal's central comparison. The revised literature table restores it and explicitly avoids claiming novelty for controlled source-representation effects.

CodeThread already compares later tasks on human/agent predecessors meeting observable test preconditions; SlopCodeBench already evaluates repeated evolution and regression/cost outcomes; NITR already tests maintainability-related structural obligations. Specification-memory and game-runtime papers add further boundaries. The revised proposal gives each a method-level consequence rather than a list of titles. Actual editions and reading extents are in the [source audit](pro-review-sources-2026-09-14.md).

**Disposition:** narrower, more informative positioning. A scoped remaining question is supported; exhaustive nonexistence of prior work is not claimed.

### R2 — Nu must pass an early case-credibility gate

Pinned source inspection confirms a material package difference: MMCC updates an explicit gameplay model with manual motion/collision logic, whereas ImSim uses persistent screen/entity properties and dynamic physics bodies. It also shows why neither is a pure paradigm representative: MMCC can invoke world effects, and both combine several mechanisms.

The old proposal correctly acknowledged physics/API asymmetry but still bundled full chain construction into Phase A. A0 now checks both baselines and at most two contrasting reference change witnesses per package before the full workload. Common observations need not demand identical physics trajectories, but they must describe equivalent selected obligations without hiding a major mismatch behind arbitrary tolerance.

**Disposition:** proceed only to a proposed model-free feasibility decision. Do not replace Nu with a stub engine or erase the organizational difference to make a test pass. Keep alternative case/domain selection possible.

### R3 — Missing cost must not erase known behavior

The original outcome paragraph grouped unknown usage with unscored correctness. A complete safe submission can have valid behavioral evidence and incomplete usage. The revised plan preserves both dimensions: retain the score, mark resources incomplete, and pause later dispatch when accounting prevents verifying remaining authorization.

A compile failure makes joint completion false; it does not establish that every individual runtime assertion was executed and failed. Future slots skipped after policy termination are not observed model failures. They may contribute zero only to an explicitly defined assigned-policy utility. Infrastructure-unobserved outcomes remain null and are bounded rather than invented.

**Disposition:** separate observed outcome, execution state, assigned-policy score and resource coverage. No retrospective rescore of existing experiments.

### R4 — Cumulative failure is not the same as a new regression

If episode 1 breaks an old obligation and episodes 2–4 never repair it, four failed checkpoints do not constitute four newly introduced regressions. New behavior not yet implemented is also different from previously working behavior lost.

The proposal now requires stable obligation identities and effective-version/supersession rules. For each episode, distinguish new behavior, retained obligations newly lost, persistent inherited failures, recoveries, superseded obligations, and outcomes blocked by compilation or missing execution. Runtime or test counts are not the unit of requirement importance.

**Disposition:** retain the convenient cumulative endpoint, but make its causal ambiguity visible through an obligation-transition record. Do not use one cumulative total as architectural explanation.

### R5 — Separate fixed-policy robustness from latent maintainability

One no-feedback submission per episode deliberately carries syntax, API and behavioral damage forward. That can answer whether the assigned package survives this restrictive policy; it cannot isolate latent architectural damage in otherwise working code. This is particularly important given E3a's earlier submission-format failures.

The revised proposal gives two legitimate options: keep the minimal case with a bounded fixed-policy claim; or predeclare a small clean-reference-predecessor sentinel control for selected later tasks. Any sentinel is separately accounted for, selected before outcomes, and compared without resetting the actual inherited arm. Do not run it only when the preferred package fails, or silently enlarge the proposed dispatch envelope.

**Disposition:** do not require a large factorial. Require either the missing contrast or the narrower claim. No success-only filtering or post-hoc manual repairs.

### R6 — Requirements should match; necessary edit effort need not

Matching observable requirements, information and candidate authority is appropriate. Forcing equal touched modules or equal coordination effort would partly remove the hypothesized architectural advantage. Conversely, avoiding that overcontrol does not license a gratuitously weak comparator or unrelated physics task.

ALMA's scenario-based reasoning supports discriminating changes and alternative-comparison goals. The revised proposal asks reviewers to predict affected responsibilities and adverse cases before candidate outcomes, distinguishing expected application changes from deliberate stress scenarios.

**Disposition:** prospective predictions make the result informative whether one package wins, loses or shows opposite behavior across chains. Neither names nor code length certify architecture quality.

### R7 — Source inheritance and notes are different memory policies

The preceding proposal supplied fresh episode conversations plus optional candidate notes. A notes file is external memory even though the conversation restarts; it can carry mistaken guidance and independently affect future outcomes.

The revised primary proposal is source-only inheritance, with earlier/current requirements supplied consistently and no separate candidate-authored notes file. Notes-on remains a possible explicit later intervention. This is a **proposed pre-adoption scientific change**, not an alteration to any existing live/frozen treatment, and must be accepted before construction/execution.

**Disposition:** remove an unnecessary memory mechanism from the first package comparison. Comments inherent in candidate source remain source; do not claim source can be stripped of all intent.

### R8 — Modest statistics and a finite research loop

The proposed 12 blocks provide repeated procedures on one pair and two chosen chains. They do not create 12 projects, 24 architectures or independent scenario families. Repeated cumulative scores are dependent. A bootstrap over identical observed block differences can collapse to a point; that is not certainty about future runs or tasks.

The revised plan keeps whole-block summaries and missing-value bounds, and explicitly labels equal-chain weights, non-executed utility values, selected task scope and any degenerate resampling. The 192+5 envelope remains unallocated, not a power calculation. A0/A1 must justify the final sample and exact resources rather than assuming every proposed number is already fixed.

**Disposition:** do not infer equivalence from non-significance, discard incomplete blocks to name a winner, or extend sampling based on the direction of the effect.

### R9 — Turn scite and literature accumulation into a decision procedure

The current records already make useful distinctions about access, versions and five-result cutoffs. This pass adds a short reusable [research-loop procedure](research-loop-and-scite.md), linked from PLAN and AGENTS. It requires question/inclusion boundaries, effective pagination, identity checks, primary-method reading, graph coverage checks, actual citation contexts, a deferred queue and a final claim-to-design consequence.

The present search itself was bounded: 40 positions of a broad 1,713-hit query and 20 positions of a noisy 86-hit query, plus exact lookups and graph checks. Its incoming graph had zero edges with low-coverage flags; its outgoing graph was truncated. No saturation or global novelty claim follows. Code-cleanliness and functional-UI bodies were unavailable through scite but accessible through primary routes. A readable access flag and citation labels were not substituted for methods.

**Disposition:** record both what changed and what remains unassessed. The decision to request A0 can be justified without claiming a full systematic review. A stronger novelty claim needs its own completed search scope.
