## A01: reconstruction and consequence

The paper asks two different questions: whether difficult future changes were foreseen, and whether its architectural categories capture change complexity. The case is Sagitta 2000/SD. The log covers September 1999–February 2001; planned evolution and technical-environment changes outside the selected log lack sufficient documentation and are not the analyzed universe. Of 117 logged requests, 61 implementation-bug requests are excluded, leaving 56. This is a selected functional-change analysis, not 117 independent behavioral trials.

The earlier thirteen scenarios map to five later requests through four scenario rows (Table 2). Table 4 cross-classifies those requests with **retrospectively applied** architectural complexity: 2 foreseen/complex, 9 unforeseen/complex, 3 foreseen/noncomplex and 42 unforeseen/noncomplex. This is not simply a prospective classifier confusion matrix. In particular, the text says CR333's scenario was originally anticipated to be noncomplex, but later dependencies made it complex.

For the second question, one experienced developer involved in implementation labels 20 requests complex, 32 noncomplex and four rejected requests unknown. Table 7 has cells 9, 2, 11, 30 and 4. These are architectural-category versus developer-label comparisons, with competing meanings of complexity, not independent measured effort or executable acceptance tests. The paper discusses organizational coordination, domain expertise, technical difficulty and missing design information as explanations for disagreements.

The source directly narrows novelty: architecture comparison is already an explicit ALMA goal in section 3.1, and subsequent-change validation is already performed here. The local extension is the behavioral value of a *coding-agent package recommendation against declared simple choices*. Methodologically, narrow change classes, inspect hidden constraints and component-local difficulty, and preserve prediction revisions. ALF retains implementation bugs because its assigned inheritance policy includes them; adopting this paper's exclusions would change that question.

Printed-count checks were recomputed from the inspected tables: 28+6+22+61=117; 117−61=56; 2+9+3+42=56; 9+2+11+30+4=56; developer known labels total 52. Agreement among those known labels is 39/52, a descriptive reconstruction, not a validated predictive-accuracy estimate or new empirical replication. The illustrative maintenance-cost expression is not used as ALF's utility or estimator; no future-change probability distribution is inferred from it.

## A02: reconstruction and consequence

This is methods guidance illustrated with cases, not an empirical validation of the ALF design. It distinguishes natural-context case studies from controlled experiments and discusses explicit case/unit boundaries, protocols, multiple evidence sources, rival explanations and construct/internal/external/reliability threats. Its appendices consolidate checklists; checking boxes cannot establish validity.

For ALF, use a fixed comparative experiment situated in a software case, with traceable source-to-prediction-to-observation reasoning. A0 remains constructive feasibility work. Deliberately selected cases and repeated model trajectories do not estimate a population of architectures. The paper's human-participant discussion does not add a recruitment or ethics gate to this no-human proposal. Section 5.1 allows quantitative analysis under appropriate design; the paper does not justify treating all local numeric comparisons as either automatically valid or categorically forbidden.

## A03: reconstruction and consequence

This is a **position paper**, not a new benchmark trial. It distinguishes ordinary error compounding, harder local work and history-dependent degradation, then proposes matched checkpoints and protocol-specific comparisons. Its benchmark tables summarize other studies; they are not extra replications.

Its product baseline requires compatible stages and conditioning. Independently chosen canonical states, alternate valid solutions, recovery and branching can violate the simple composition. Revealing goals, replacing code, resetting history and changing budgets are distinct interventions. Its proposed log contrast diagnoses a mismatch relative to the chosen protocol, not the mechanism causing it. These points reinforce ALF's direct sentinel comparison and claim boundary; no residual or extra condition is adopted.

A local algebra check found a caveat in the prose following equation (4), PDF 6: positive covariance between log checkpoint and log rollout estimates does not by itself make the independence approximation understate variance. For `U−V`, variance is `Var(U)+Var(V)−2Cov(U,V)`; positive cross-covariance alone lowers it, while covariance among checkpoint terms can raise it. With variances 1 and 1 and covariance 0.5, the difference variance is 1 versus the independence value 2. This limits that directional assertion, not the entire position. ALF does not import the estimator or its uncertainty approximation as validated machinery.
