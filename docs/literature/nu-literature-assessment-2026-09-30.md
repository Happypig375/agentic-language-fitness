## Three criteria and the next decision

**Unique:** priority remains unconfirmed. Pure world transformations, reactive dependencies, replay, temporal tests, live editing and game tooling all have close predecessors. The credible contribution would be a new, bounded empirical result about a specified organization, obligation family and maintainer policy. D1 has its own narrower overlap analysis; neither naming Nu nor narrowing a question proves firstness.

**Valuable:** the beneficiary and possible decisions are concrete: a maintainer could choose a convention, pay for observation/verification, or avoid a convention for adverse change families. Effect size, frequency of those changes, learning/support costs and net payoff remain unknown. The literature already justifies retaining a null/adverse result and comparing ordinary defaults, source access and useful feedback fairly.

**Scientifically valid:** the literature supports a credible design direction, not a validated apparatus. Any later study must specify initial equivalence, information/tool access, independent all-obligation tests, source/effect boundaries, task-family dependence, all assigned attempts and complete cost accounting. Hints, correct-only timing, post-outcome case filtering and unmodeled repeated observations in earlier studies are controls to address, not methods to copy. Actual edit size, repair count and observed context traffic are outcomes, not preassignment covariates that isolate architecture.

The next decision is **human disposition of the question and a separately scoped model-free feasibility packet**, with the existing D1 recommendation available and the broader Nu mechanisms retained as motivation/adverse cases. This assessment does not select a new treatment, construct A0/A1, recruit participants or authorize a worker/model call. New allocation stays zero. The existing A0 cap, transport/backend decisions and historical results remain unchanged.
