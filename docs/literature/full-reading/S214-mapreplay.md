## What the method preserves and transforms

MapReplay sits between isolated operation microbenchmarks and complete applications. It modifies `HashMap` source, deploys the replacement through a `java.base` patch, and records selected events through JNI/native code. This avoids having the recorder recursively invoke the Java collections being instrumented. Offline processing removes unsupported events and maps whose construction was not recorded, compacts traversal and assigns storage slots. Replay interprets compact primitive arrays and invokes the selected HashMap operations. Mock keys and trace arrays are prepared outside timed replay; maps and iterators are allocated during replay.

The intended fidelity is **the library workload relevant to the tested performance change**, not complete application behavior. The method records operation order, map identity and key hashes, with canonicalization of equal keys. It tries to retain bucket collisions, occupancy and operation mixes. Some operations are replayed directly; other state-changing operations are represented through simpler effects. Values and application callbacks are replaced or omitted. Key equality/hash stability is assumed. Value-sensitive operations such as `containsValue`, arbitrary key comparison costs and callback work are outside the preserved workload.

Other boundaries are explicit in §§3 and 6:

- Replay is single-threaded, including traces from parallel applications. Correctly synchronized shared mutable maps and independent/read-only map operations motivate the ordering argument. This is not a reconstruction of synchronization costs or a method for concurrent collections.
- Replay sites use one map type, permitting monomorphic JIT optimization. Application polymorphism, surrounding allocation/GC pressure, cache interactions and contention are not reproduced.
- Traces omit exceptional operations, and the paper assumes exception-free replay. Its characterization of such operations as rare or indicative of bugs does not prove all exceptional Java paths have no state effects.
- Traversal operations are coalesced; object references are cleared at last recorded use, rather than the application's actual lifetime. Hence the recorded operation stream does not preserve all dispatch costs or retained memory.
- Preserving hashes is insufficient to preserve ordering inside treeified buckets. The authors explicitly leave comparison relationships and tree-based collections for further work. Extending the approach to other collections is a proposal with implementation obligations.

The source check makes these conditions more concrete. The public release deletes `tryAdvance`/`trySplit` events, retains a JDK-sensitive patch, and supplies no independent result-equivalence oracle. Map-local key identifiers are globally interned by identifier/hash in replay; a constructed cross-map `putAll` case can merge distinct original keys. This is a source-level counterexample to universal fidelity, not a measured error in the paper's workloads. Published correlations remain evidence under their stated conditions; they do not establish that every transformed trace is semantically equivalent.

## Evaluation and retained results

The case study changes HashMap's **default initial capacity (DIC)** from 16 to 32, 64 or 128. Larger capacity can reduce resizing and collisions but increase allocation and sparse-table iteration costs. This is one configuration family, not a comparison of persistent versus mutable representations or an architecture-wide intervention.

Table 1 lists **43 application workloads** from DaCapo-Chopin and Renaissance. Seven are map-intensive, with at least 5% of CPU time attributed to HashMap; 27 are map-moderate, with less than 5% CPU but at least 100,000 processed events. Nine map-minimal workloads are excluded from the performance comparison. These categories are derived from the table and the paper's stated rule, not a sample-size claim about independent systems. The seven intensive cases include two related mnemonic workloads. C-jme has 0.02% CPU and 11,189 processed events and is among the excluded cases; its presence in the trace inventory does not make it an evaluated intensive workload.

The reported machine is a 16-core Xeon Gold 6326 at 2.9 GHz, 256 GB RAM, Ubuntu 22.04/kernel 5.15, with hyper-threading and turbo disabled, using Oracle JDK 25.0.1+8-LTS-27. JMH 1.37 uses five forks, five warmup and five measurement iterations. Microbenchmark iterations last 500 ms; replay iterations last 10 seconds. Application measurements use 30 JVM runs and workload-dependent 30–100 iterations. The table's printed ± values are not independently assigned a statistic here: the acquired release lacks the analysis/results binding needed to resolve their exact calculation.

Other concrete contrasts matter: at DIC64, C-biojava improves by about 1.13× in replay but only 1.02× in the application; R-fj-kmeans improves by about 1.18× in replay while the application is almost unchanged. At DIC128, R-neo4j-analytics falls to about 0.76× in replay with little application change. These retain both library-local sensitivity and the substantial dilution or transformation of application-level effects.

The binomial calculation in §5.5 requires a narrower interpretation than the paper's prose suggests. A one-sided p of approximately 0.000745 is correct for 18 or more agreements in 21 independent trials with agreement probability 0.5. **Independence of the two direction variables alone does not imply agreement probability 0.5.** Their observed positive counts are 13/21 and 16/21; plugging these marginal proportions into the independence formula gives `(13×16 + 8×5)/21² = 0.56236`. This is an illustration of the omitted assumption, not a replacement inferential analysis. The three changes within each workload also share a baseline, and workloads are not all unrelated. Keep the observed agreement; do not interpret the quoted p-value as a general accuracy guarantee across independent optimization tasks.

## Artifact correspondence and survey consequence

The [release check](S214-mapreplay-artifact-check.md) finds **46 published preprocessed traces**, not an inaccessible or absent dataset. Their filenames cover all 43 table workloads plus three extra DaCapo cases. The release launcher selects all trace ZIPs by default, whereas the paper's comparison excludes nine of its 43 cases. One acquired JME member reports **8,603 events and 463 constructors**, compared with the paper's **11,189 events and 911 constructors**. This identifies a material lineage discrepancy; its cause is not established. Trace inputs are not raw timing results, and no acquired source/release inventory supplies the full application-run, DIC-patch and statistical-analysis recipe. The complete 1.96 GB archive and executable are not acquired or run.

For **B06/B12**, this is a constructive methodological alternative to treating isolated collection timings as application benefit. Application-derived traces can retain operation distributions and expose changes otherwise diluted by unrelated work. The positive agreement and reduced measurement schedule are worth preserving. An evaluation still has to specify what the trace omits, its edition, retention behavior, cost accounting and the application outcome it predicts. For **B07**, replay agreement is neither an independent temporal oracle nor evidence that pending work, external effects or behavior under changed schedules has been checked.

For **B04/B06**, this qualifies transfer from [S211](S211-rrb-systems-persistence.md), [S212](S212-persistent-iterators.md) and [S213](S213-transient-sequence.md): realistic operation/alias/retention distributions matter, and fidelity itself needs evidence. It supplies no measured Nu, F#/.NET, persistent-history or developer-maintenance benefit. Those empirical residuals should remain separate from unread primary methods. Continue the existing .NET/game/practice and type/evolution frontiers according to which can change the survey's conclusions; no benchmark construction or execution follows from this reading.
