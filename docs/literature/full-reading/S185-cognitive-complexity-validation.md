## What is being validated

Cognitive Complexity assigns increments to control-flow interruptions and nesting, while omitting selected shorthand and structural elements. The Figure 1 example counts a switch as a whole rather than each case. This is a source metric with a design rationale; it is not a count of behavioral obligations, semantic alternatives or checked temporal properties. The authors explicitly distinguish earlier acceptance of suggested fixes from empirical validation of understandability.

The paper asks whether the score is associated with human comprehension time, answer correctness, perceived ease, physiological measures and a constructed time/correctness outcome. Its unit of association is an aggregated **snippet observation within a dataset**, rather than each individual human response. It does not manipulate Cognitive Complexity while holding behavior constant, estimate the effect of refactoring to lower it, validate an F# case-enumeration rule, or measure long-term maintenance of an interactive system.

## Search, data and analysis reconstruction

The review searches Google Scholar, ACM, IEEE and ScienceDirect for English peer-reviewed work from 2010 to July 2019, using title-focused comprehension/readability terms, then performs one backward-snowball step without the year restriction. One author screens and another validates. Figure 2 traces 7,068 initial returns through type/year/deduplication filters to 2,357 screened entries and 28 relevant papers; snowballing adds 1,053 entries before further deduplication and eligibility checks. Availability of source snippets and human measurements constrains inclusion. Of twelve author contacts, six reportedly replied and three supplied data. These are the paper's actions, not contacts made in this survey.

Ten usable datasets contribute **427 snippet entries and about 24,000 human evaluations**. The **nine datasets/327 entries** appearing in the earlier search passage are the **time subset**, not a competing edition or an independent replication. Languages are Java, C/C++, Scala, C# and JavaScript. Participants include students and professionals; tasks include output prediction, questions, cloze tests, ratings and bug finding. Participant counts in Table 2 and the released descriptions sometimes differ; recruited versus analyzed populations are not fully reconciled. Do not sum them into a count of unique independent people.

The authors copy snippets, repair extraction artifacts and add imports, dummy classes or missing brackets as needed for SonarQube, claiming no added control structures. The supplement does not redistribute all original code because of its licenses. Most code is short and has low metric values. Study 6 includes 50 Java snippets with median 7.5 and maximum 46; study 9 includes 30 Java snippets with median 4 and maximum 16. The released study-8 data also include values above 15, despite the paper's descriptive summary; the artifact note locates this discrepancy precisely.

Human observations are aggregated per snippet. Most associations use Kendall's tau, transformed by `r = sin(pi*tau/2)`; two selected variables use Pearson correlation. Correlations enter a Fisher-z random-effects meta-analysis. The released R source uses `meta`/`metacor`, `method.tau="SJ"`; it averages some within-study correlations arithmetically before pooling and does not specify their covariance. The paper's discussion of aggregation and Equation 1's median-time notation do not establish a uniform raw-observation transformation across the heterogeneous source studies.

## Positive, null and conflicting evidence

The five published forest plots and their released images agree. Signs below refer to the plotted outcome, so a negative relationship with ease ratings is consistent with greater perceived difficulty at higher complexity.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Comprehension time | 9 / 327 | **.54 [.24, .75]** | I² 85%, tau² .2553 | Preserve the positive average relationship, with large task/dataset variation |
| Answer correctness | 6 / 269 | **−.13 [−.45, .21]** | I² 79%, tau² .1542 | Mixed results; interval crosses zero and allows relationships in both directions |
| Perceived ease/readability | 4 / 203 | **−.29 [−.49, −.06]** | I² 46%, tau² .035 | Preserve a modest average association with subjective judgments |
| Physiological measures | 1 study / 12 snippets / 3 brain-area outcomes | **.00 [−.37, .37]** | I² 0%, tau² .0085 | Limited evidence from one small dataset; the plot's summed 36 is repeated outcomes |
| Constructed time/correctness score | 6 / 269 | **.40 [.11, .62]** | I² 78%, tau² .1161 | Positive association with a derived score, not another independent human endpoint |

Time correlations range from −.03 and .08 in two professional-task datasets to .94 and .80 in side-effect/reactive-program tasks. Correctness includes an opposing +.57 in the cloze dataset, alongside negative and near-zero results. Those differences are part of the evidence, not observations to discard to strengthen an overall claim. Neither a pooled nonzero estimate nor a large I² establishes the cause of variation.

Equation 1 combines normalized time and correctness as `(T / Tmax) * (1 - C / Cmax)`, with maxima taken from the observed dataset/subgroup. The released 269 values match this formula under our elementary arithmetic check. When the best observed correctness is below perfection, that best observation still has a zero score regardless of time. For example, the released maxima are about .815 in study 6 and .651 in study 9. The composite therefore embodies a particular relative tradeoff; its association cannot replace separate reporting of time and correctness.

## Dependence, release correspondence and transfer

The supplement explicitly identifies study 2 as using a subset of study 1's code; all twelve released snippet identifiers and metric values coincide. Human measurements may differ, but code samples are not independent. Within studies, some repeated time/rating outcomes also share snippets and participants. The three physiological rows refer to the same twelve snippets and are passed separately to the meta-analysis. The source averages selected correlations and sums disjoint subgroup sizes without a general multilevel/covariance model. These facts qualify the precision and interpretation of the pooled evidence without erasing its observed associations.

The most concrete release discrepancy is bounded: study 8 has **40 measurement rows but 19 distinct snippet-label strings**, with several labels reused across different metric values. Its all-row metric summary is median **5**, maximum **23**, SD **5.169**. The source's separate descriptive routine keeps only the first row per study/snippet label, giving **19 rows**, median **4**, maximum **14**, SD **3.603**, exactly corresponding to Table 2's descriptive values. Its correlation routine retains all forty rows. This is evidence about a descriptive filter and ambiguous identifiers, not a recalculated effect size or proof that only nineteen programs were studied. Four study-8 rows exceed 15, so the literal statement that only two datasets exceed that threshold is not supported by the release; the broader low-complexity concentration remains.

The paper establishes neither a validated threshold of 15 nor incremental predictive value over LOC or other metrics on a common held-out dataset. Its recommendations to keep scores low go beyond its observational design if read as causal promises. Comparisons with metrics from other publications do not provide a controlled ranking when tasks, samples and outcomes differ.

## Consequence for Nu and the survey

For B01/B11/B12, Cognitive Complexity has **scoped positive human evidence**, especially for time and perceived ease; it should not be dismissed as a metric with no validation. Its uneven correctness evidence, low-range sampling, aggregation and dependence prevent treating it as a substitute for independent behavior or maintenance outcomes. [S181](S181-displayed-metric-anchoring.md)'s equal-score selection does not imply equally difficult tasks, while displayed-score anchoring does not refute S185's undisplayed source-score association. These studies answer different questions.

For Nu, metric changes can be descriptive or diagnostic evidence under an explicit construct. They do not establish the benefit of persistence, message architecture, exhaustive case enumeration, agent repair, compact source or live evolution. The next consequential gap is B02's algebraic-model/interface alternative, **GATlab**, DOI [10.46298/entics.14666](https://doi.org/10.46298/entics.14666): reconstruct what theory maps actually migrate before treating that claim as live-state evolution. Runtime, practice and temporal-oracle residuals remain distinct; no experiment is authorized.
