## Live claim and design

B01/B05/B06/B10/B11/B12 concern whether finer-grained adaptable organization necessarily improves maintenance, whether component replacement offers an alternative, and how runtime measurements differ from maintenance predictions. The single industrial case is an Ericsson fraud-control application's C++ parser. Four people were interviewed separately: its main designer, parser implementer, another project programmer and a designer from another project in the same product domain. These are purposively chosen informants, not four independent projects or maintenance trials.

The original parser implements a State pattern with nine dynamically constructed state objects, each involving regular-expression machinery. The proposed alternative uses Lex/Yacc plus C++, with a specialized replaceable parser for each input format. It also changes concurrency from threads to UNIX processes, described as one coordinator plus eight workers. Thus generation, representation, allocation behavior and concurrency isolation change together. The comparison cannot isolate the effect of object orientation, patterns, code size, or a source-level convention.

A project-informed person supplied fourteen maintenance scenarios. The modified architecture-assessment method graded changed and new code on an ordinal scale and summed scenario impacts. Although scenario weighting appears in the method's steps, the paper does not publish the complete scenario profile, weights, numeric category mapping or resulting scores. This assessment selected the alternative; it is not a held-out validation of a selector or an observed maintenance-task comparison.

One developer implemented the alternative from the architecture strategy and original requirements, with instructions to make it quickly and simply. The paper reports no prior professional Lex/Yacc experience for this developer. It explicitly says the prototype omitted design documentation and received limited verification. Common requirements therefore do not establish behavioral equivalence or equal production readiness.

The maintenance argument combines an asserted size–effort association with new-code and legacy-modification productivity figures from different cited studies. A large size difference does not itself validate transporting those rates across languages, teams, tasks and generated code. Likewise, retrospective statements that the old parser was easy to adapt do not quantify the alternative's maintenance benefit. Neither the fourteen-scenario assessment nor the initial-development comparison supplies the missing prospective outcome.

## Printed quantities and reproducibility limits

The endpoint ratios above were recalculated from Table 2, not from rerunning a benchmark. Figure 5's table contains CPU counts 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 and throughput numbers `18 … 6.8` versus `68 … 556`. Its axis says only “Throughput”: these values are a factor of 100 below the event/s endpoints in the prose and Table 2, without an explicit unit multiplier. Relative ratios agree; do not silently treat the graph's numbers as absolute event/s.

The prose calls the allocation counts 100 and eleven per **event file**, while Table 2 writes `100*event + 84` and `11*event + 19`. The counted unit and initialization terms therefore need clarification before deriving total allocation savings. The paper argues allocator contention, but does not separate a reduced-allocation intervention from the process/thread change in this comparison. An allocator replacement is itself a relevant alternative mentioned in the paper and earlier work.

No parser sources, input corpus, full fourteen-scenario matrix, raw timing runs or interview transcripts were obtained. No parser, benchmark, maintenance experiment or allocator was executed. Missing uncertainty and equivalence evidence are not repaired by the large reported ratio.

## Publication lineage and implications

[S80](S80-maintainability-performance-lineage.md) repeats the same project, interview responses, 3,889/433 source lines, development times, allocation expressions and throughput series. It is a shorter publication of this empirical case, not an independent replication. S79 gives additional interview selection, scenario-assessment and discussion detail. Count two fully read publications and one underlying case.

**Unique:** comparing organization using concrete change scenarios and considering whole-component replacement has clear prior art. This is not a functional-versus-object-oriented or coding-agent experiment; the narrower Nu/D1 distinction remains unconfirmed. **Valuable:** implementation and runtime mechanisms can overturn an intuitive recommendation, while future maintenance benefit still requires outcome evidence. **Scientifically valid:** separate source-based predictions, initial construction effort, runtime measurements and actual maintenance success; retain comparator readiness and combined-treatment limits. No experimental hold is lifted.
