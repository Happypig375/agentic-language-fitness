## Definition and scope

The authors distinguish two dimensions that are often conflated. **Hot fixing** addresses an unplanned, time-critical production problem; **hot patching** updates a running system while preserving processes/sessions. An urgent repair can require a restart, and a live update can be planned. A patching mechanism therefore does not by itself establish an urgent-repair workflow or its benefit.

Section 12.1 operationalizes the definition through three jointly required conditions: a reactive production-incident trigger, a severely constrained development/mitigation window, and release at the earliest operationally viable moment. Severity/urgency is context-dependent, including business impact, rather than one fixed duration or defect label. Table 17 distinguishes an incident repair from an urgent feature deadline or immediate routine improvement. These are proposed classification rules, not a validated universal labeling oracle.

The review covers critical-issue detection, remediation, deployment and empirical practice. It admits transferable techniques even when authors do not use hot-fix terminology. General runtime updating without the criticality condition is excluded, as is post-deployment monitoring considered independently of hot fixing. Its reported overlap with Islam et al.'s runtime-patching survey is 8/140 papers. This scope is useful for incident response, but it cannot close Nu's broader live-editing, state-migration or temporal-observation background. The review's later call for post-deployment validation also reaches an area excluded from its own search scope.

## What the evidence contributes

The review connects urgent repair to an operational sequence: notice and triage a problem, determine a feasible intervention, deliver it, and subsequently check its effect. It exposes useful alternatives and constraints rather than establishing one best architecture:

| Mechanism or evidence family | Contribution retained | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Practice and release cadence | Studies describe rapid corrective commits, emergency app updates that can persist, urgent game updates, and stable rapid deployment in particular organizations | Definitions include temporal proxies and developer labels; rates from different populations/release policies are not a common prevalence estimate or causal cadence effect |
| Incident classification and diagnosis | Monitoring, recurring-incident signatures, log/KPI clustering, alert prioritization and guide recommendation have reported production applications and positive accuracy/response findings | Diagnosis accuracy, adoption, restoration time and correct repair are different outcomes; a tool deployed in production is not thereby evaluated against a common counterfactual |
| Configuration and containment | Reconfiguration can address problems without new source code; symptom mitigation can restore availability when root-cause repair is too slow | Recovery may sacrifice functionality/performance or leave the defect in place. Some reported Mandelbug probabilities/times are expert/model estimates rather than an observed incident distribution |
| Patch generation | Offline and runtime approaches target security, memory, concurrency or particular platforms, with concrete successful cases | Author patch availability, bug class and test/oracle access constrain applicability; the category name alone does not certify semantic preservation |
| Deployment and recovery alternatives | Runtime patching, selective binary updates, rolling upgrades, VM-preserving host updates, restartability and microreboots are distinct ways to reduce disruption | Code delivery, state consistency, side effects, rollback and retained functionality still require separate evidence |
| Human work | User/admin studies describe decision, preparation, deployment and remedy stages, plus information and organizational burdens | Neither tool counts nor algorithmic latency measures represent total work under an urgent production constraint |

Positive and adverse results remain at the review's reported scope. For example, Table 14 reports45/49 Apache bugs amenable to the studied runtime patching, while selective-update validation succeeds for 19 of 21 developer patches and fails for 2. Table 15 distinguishes ClearView detecting all 10 evaluated exploits from generating corrective patches for 7. Table 11's Rx result covers all **six** tested failures across four server applications, not all possible failures. Table 13 reports 55/373 Android CVEs handled by VULMET. These are heterogeneous review-level summaries, not our independently reconstructed primary experiments or quantities to combine into a success rate.

The adverse account matters as well: symptom mitigation can remove features; failure recovery can cause additional failures; deployment mechanisms can introduce security exposure; infrastructure/dependency/configuration incidents may require operational remedies instead of source patches. The review's suggestion that hot fixes contribute to architecture degeneration is an inference from a compiler-architecture study, not a measured hot-fix causal effect. Preserve useful successful interventions without treating rapid recovery as complete correctness or all hot fixes as damaging.

## Relationship to this survey and later evidence

The authors' statement that large hot-fix benchmarks were not found is scoped to their search and date. [S188](S188-hotbugs-urgent-repair.md), the 8 October 2025 HotBugs.jar preprint already reconstructed here, supplies 679 urgent-fix records and reports 110 integrated cases; the passive artifact check distinguishes 109 new integrations from an inherited case and preserves test/version limitations. It changes that dated benchmark gap without establishing live deployment, complete temporal obligations or a universally representative benchmark.

Likewise, [S162](S162-industry-hotfix-workflows.md) already reconstructs the 2026 mixed-method journal extension of *Behind the Hot Fix*, which is reference 54 here. Its reused160-person survey plus 9 interviews supplies developer/operational accounts and explicit post-deployment verification. Those data are not independent replications of the precursor. They refine the review's developer-practice gap and supply broader workflow evidence than the three-phase tooling taxonomy.

S162's selected respondents report automation more often in verification/deployment than detection/generation. S187 finds more **publications** tagged for detection than deployment. These are compatible observations with different denominators, populations and measures; neither should overwrite the other or be treated as a causal priority ranking. Our B04/B08/B10/B12 synthesis therefore separates research attention, reported workplace automation and measured intervention benefit.
