## Contribution and actual evidence

This explicitly extends S46, adding monadic stream functions, software/hardware-in-the-loop testing and fault injection to temporal predicates, generated traces, assertions and replay. Repeated bouncing-ball, Haskanoid and debugger examples are not independent replications. Pure signal functions preserve deterministic computation when their initial computation, full input samples, sample schedules and architecture assumptions are held fixed. Monadic extensibility permits controlled effects; it does not make arbitrary external state referentially transparent.

Figures 1–3 show sampling-sensitive graph layouts and stream-function composition; figures 4–5 show the debugger and replayed failure; figure 6 embeds hardware in a stream network; figure 7 is a schematic satellite demonstration with separation diagnostics. These are mechanism illustrations, not comparative developer outcomes. The empirical material consists of reported counterexamples, capability demonstrations and author experience. There is no controlled human/agent maintenance comparison or measured net benefit. Commercial-game use is disclosed as involving the first author's company.

The bounded evaluation is consequential. Short 60 Hz traces pass one hundred tests before an expanded run finds a failure after 897. The simplified Haskanoid physics example passes one hundred cases around 25 FPS before a displayed larger run fails after 3,443; a speed predicate finds a counterexample after 67. These repeat S46's examples and are individual runs, not sensitivity estimates. The system model omits full-game features; the repair is explicitly ad hoc. Low-level generators are not expected to produce complete realistic gameplay, so recorded prefixes and generated continuations are combined.

## Temporal logic and oracle limits

The conceptual time domain is continuous and unbounded; tests observe finite sampled streams. `evalT` queries the initial time, while modalities refer to other points. Section 3.3 provides its type and interpretation, not the complete evaluator implementation. The paper deliberately returns true for `Imminently`/`After` beyond the observed domain, acknowledging that an unknown result would be more literal. Thus a passing test does not establish a pending future obligation. Live past-time assertions and completed-trace future-time queries are distinct capabilities.

Sampling assumptions are not captured by the stated properties in the principal approach. The same continuous model may behave differently under different numerical sampling. Snapshotting can also discard the history required to interpret a temporal assertion. Source equivalence, final behavioral scoring and histories therefore need an independent, explicit oracle; the printed predicate vocabulary alone cannot supply one.

Three discrepancies were verified in rendered publication pages. Page 12 transposes conjunction/disjunction symbols relative to the function names; page 27's Haskell operators use the conventional directions. Page 13 repeats S46's problematic Until equation: an existential future-time constraint appears as an implication, and the inner interval lacks its lower bound. At any query time after zero, an earlier witness makes the displayed implication true even when both predicates are always false. Page 28's logger acts when the supplied holding-condition is true although the prose describes reporting violations. These are publication discrepancies, not evidence that a particular experimental executable behaved that way.

## Effects, costs and external validity

Sections 6–7 require both correct communication and an external reset operation before each test. That reset obligation is assumed, not enforced by the temporal language. Without it, a counterexample and its shrinking may be irreproducible. The Arduino demonstration restricts input to positive numbers and simplifies representation; passing its one hundred generated tests does not validate arbitrary inputs or device behavior. The paper's claim about a parser's negative-input support was not independently established here; an official documentation request returned no usable body and is not credited.

The satellite model changes simulated physics and control frequency with the same clock. The authors acknowledge this prevents identifying which caused a changed outcome. Reported fault rates and randomized seeds define specific simulations; redundant sensing is not a general fault-tolerance guarantee. The hidden random seed also limits shrinking. Different clocks and stronger fault models remain claim-triggered follow-ups.

Replay after a code edit means recompilation, restart and replay, not automatic migration of arbitrary saved state. Retained intermediate signal functions support navigation, while input-only recording requires recomputation. Arbitrary-start snapshot recording, portable external-state restoration and property-history preservation are separate issues. UI edits can invalidate low-level traces. Recording overhead is unmeasured and may prevent a fault from appearing at all; input-only logging mitigates but does not quantify that risk.

## Three criteria and remaining frontier

**Unique:** this is direct prior art for functional game replay, temporal testing, generated continuations and effectful testing under reset assumptions. Broad mechanism-firstness is untenable; any specific Nu convention/lifecycle question remains unconfirmed. **Valuable:** reproducibility and temporal observation offer plausible actions, but comparative maintenance effort, adoption and Nu/agent transfer remain unmeasured. **Scientifically valid:** a useful study must establish source equivalence, test the temporal oracle independently, retain sampling/history/effect boundaries and unsuccessful attempts, and account for instrumentation and reset cost. Allocation remains zero.

The earlier untruncated five-edge incoming graph and exact-identity follow-up are reused, not resubmitted as fresh screening. S53 remains promoted for asynchronous temporal semantics. Rhine, fault-tolerant FRP, automatic requirement-to-monitor translation and FACT remain conditional on adopting those clocks, fault models or specification-generation claims. The bibliography identifies relevant logic/coverage/replay foundations, but citation by this paper is not a full reading of each source. S50 already provides an independent finite-partial-trace comparison. The broader Nu frontier still includes S54/S55, S59 and primary game-practice/replay follow-ups; this paper's completion does not close it.
