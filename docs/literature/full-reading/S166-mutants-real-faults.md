## Population and retrospective construction

Five Java projects: JFreeChart, Closure Compiler, Commons Math, Joda-Time and Commons Lang, with printed totals **321 KLOC / 211 test KLOC / 20,109 tests** for their most recent versions. Commit/issue references identify **1,179 candidate revision pairs**, reduced to **836 compilable**, **437 reproducible**, and **357 isolated faults**. Per-project final counts are 26/133/106/27/65. Build/configuration fixes judged trivial were allowed; cases needing nontrivial repairs, lacking an appropriate triggering test, or inseparable from other changes were excluded.

For each retained case, V2 is the fixed revision and V1 is constructed by reversing only the isolated bug-fix diff into V2. Thus V1 need not equal the historical parent revision. Two authors reviewed each isolation, with a third resolving disagreements. This is reported within-study author review, not independent external validation.

Developer suite pairs are built from the fixed-version suite, not simply the untouched historical before/after suites. Broken/configuration outcomes are removed; each pair differs by one added or strengthened triggering test. Both pass on V2, while only the stronger suite fails on V1 due to the known fault. **480 pairs** represent **357 faults**; several faults have multiple triggers and share nearly all suite content. These are not 480 independent projects or randomly assigned augmentation decisions.

## Generated suites, mutation and observation scope

Generation is directed to classes changed by the **known fix**. Per fixed version, EvoSuite attempts thirty suites for each of branch, weak-mutation and strong-mutation targets; Randoop attempts six each with/without null inputs; JCrasher attempts ten. Tests that do not compile or fail on V2 are removed. Nondeterministic tests are iteratively removed until the suite passes five consecutive times; about 2% become empty. This operational stability screen is not proof of no flakiness, and its exclusions cannot be imported into ISE's all-assigned-attempt denominator.

The retained **35,141 generated suites** detect **198/357 distinct faults**. EvoSuite has 28,318 retained suites and detects 182/354 faults for which it has a suitable suite; Randoop 3,387 and 90/326; JCrasher 3,436 and 2/350. These unequal eligibility denominators, generation budgets and oracle styles preclude a simple equal-cost tool ranking. JCrasher seeks unexpected crashes rather than the regression assertions used by the other generators.

Major generates about **230,000 mutants**, again only in fixed-version classes modified by the bug fix. It replaces constants/operators, changes conditions and deletes statements. Cobertura measures statement coverage over the same selected classes. Equivalent mutants are not removed; the authors argue their identical presence across compared suites permits relative comparison. That argument does not prove arbitrary mutant weighting, redundancy or absolute-score validity.

For generated-suite comparisons, the coverage-controlled mutation score is computed only over the **intersection of mutants reached by both suites**. Pairs with an empty intersection are discarded. This controls opportunity for that pair by changing its scored mutant population; it does not hold one global coverage vector fixed across all suites or establish causal independence from every aspect of test generation and size. Known fix locations and future triggering tests make this a retrospective proxy-validity study, not a prospective selector with decision-time information.

Section 2.6.4 explicitly says a coverage-independent correlation coefficient is **not** computed: fixed coverage across all suites would be needed. The later boxed conclusion uses broader language. Preserve the positive controlled score contrasts without relabeling them as such a coefficient or a causal treatment effect. Shared test suites, generators, historical fixes and project families also limit independent replication counts.

The 63 difficult cases include algorithm changes (37), code deletion (7), related internal methods (5), context-sensitive behavior (4), library contracts (3), numerical errors (4) and specific literals (3). The examples show practical weaknesses of the studied operators, including a caller context whose callee mutants are already killed elsewhere. They do not establish that these defects could never be represented by a domain-specific or higher-order mutant. The paper's headline **73% / 10% / 17%** split should not replace its exact **262 / 32 / 63** counts: dividing by 357 gives approximately **73.39% / 8.96% / 17.65%**, respectively. This arithmetic difference does not change the qualitative distinction.

The conclusion itself warns that mutation-preserving test selection/minimization can reduce real-fault detection. That warning and the observed uncoupled cases belong beside the positive additional sensitivity. Larger samples do not automatically remove selection bias, and the authors' argument from linked issues and reproducibility does not make this a population sample of all production defects, configuration errors, temporal effects or newly requested behavior.

**Unique:** mutation-guided evaluation, retrospective coupling and independent fault checks are established precedents. **Valuable:** the observed additional sensitivity is useful evidence for test assessment, with real cost and scope limits rather than a complete oracle guarantee. **Scientifically valid:** reconstruction supports bounded retrospective associations; future-information scope, selection, dependent pairs, changed score denominators and edition/artifact gaps constrain transfer. Neither reading nor criticism authorizes a new test framework, benchmark or experimental allocation.
