## Live question and theoretical argument

B01/B10/B11/B12 concern whether a prescribed source convention demonstrably improves modification, and whether a metric ranking is evidence of that improvement. This study directly manipulates **one dimension of coupling**, parameters versus global communication. It does not test all information hiding, modularity, mutable versus immutable state, or Nu's architecture.

The opening distinguishes interface size, control versus data, simple items versus structures, and global versus parameter transmission. In a simple one-level call tree, the authors define coupling as system-level Halstead effort minus summed module effort, with effort `E = V²/V*`. Their derivation assumes unchanged subordinate-module effort, common potential volume, unchanged vocabularies and additional parameter-list tokens at the caller. Under those assumptions, the parameter variant receives the higher coupling value. Other proposed measures penalize globals or treat both forms equally. This is a disagreement among metric definitions, not a proof that global state improves human performance or that these measures predict actual modification cost.

## Three experiments in one program family

The source is an approximately 500-line PL/I machine simulator originally written by students using parameters. A hand conversion replaces parameter communication with globals while retaining algorithm and module structure. The paper calls the variants equivalent; this reading could not independently verify equivalence or test sensitivity. All experiments use the same two changes: adding AND/OR instructions and adding indirect addressing. Participants are upper-level undergraduate/graduate students at one university. Three cohorts/protocols are reported, not three independent applications; individual identities are unavailable for checking cross-course participant overlap.

**Experiment 1, winter 1982:** 24 students initially, 20 retained, in four five-person cells crossing interface type and task order. Every person performs both changes on one variant. Cells were balanced by class year, PL/I ability, programming experience and card/terminal use; randomization is not explicitly established. One person per cell used cards. Work occurred outside class, with self-recorded study/decision time separated from keyboard/keypunch editing time. Participants continued until a specified input/output test passed and only then received the second task. Reasons and outcomes for the four lost participants are not supplied. The analyzed times therefore do not include all original assignments.

**Experiment 2, summer 1982:** 33 initially, 30 after course attrition, with cell sizes 6/7/8/9; the text does not map those four sizes explicitly to the table rows. Everyone used terminals. The first task was due after five days and the second after another week. A closed-book fifteen-question comprehension test occurred between changes. Delay since finishing the first task varied from one hour to four days. Time remained self-reported and work could span multiple sessions.

**Experiment 3, autumn 1982:** 21 students, eleven globals/ten parameters, all doing AND/OR first and indirect addressing second. The earlier PL/I external procedures required explicit `EXTERNAL` declarations even for global interfaces. This experiment instead uses internal procedures with lexical inheritance, making shared inputs less explicit. A Wylbur command script records study/edit phases while the experimenter supervises groups of at most four. Each task has one two-hour session, and comprehension is tested immediately after the first change. Source visibility, scheduling, task-order design, timing and supervision change together. Lower times across experiments cannot isolate a supervision or recording effect.

## Reconstructed timings and inference

The tables report study/decision time, called think time, rather than total elapsed labor including editing. The following table preserves task order and each cohort's rounded means.

| Experiment and order | Global total think minutes, first / second | Parameter total think minutes, first / second |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1: AND/OR → indirect | 105 / 95 | 38 / 145 |
| 1: indirect → AND/OR | 133 / 33 | 123 / 31 |
| 2: AND/OR → indirect | 64 / 126 | 56 / 102 |
| 2: indirect → AND/OR | 156 / 28 | 194 / 25 |
| 3: AND/OR → indirect | 26 / 44 | 29 / 43 |

In experiment 1, parameters substantially reduce the first AND/OR mean (105 to 38 minutes), with reported t(8) = 2.725/significance level .03. The coupling×task×order interaction is reported significant only for total think time, F(1,16) = 4.77/level .05, among the three outcomes. There is no significant main coupling effect. The global advantage on the subsequent indirect task is not statistically supported. These findings depend on task and prior exposure; they are not a uniform parameter advantage.

Experiment 2 does **not** reproduce the initial AND/OR difference: t(12) = .507/level .62 for total think time. Its later indirect-task means favor parameters, reversing experiment 1's direction, again without significance. Comprehension means are 10.7/11.6 for the global task-order groups and 11.0/11.8 for the parameter groups, out of fifteen; no coupling effect is detected. Comprehension and delay correlate at r = −.383/level .04. Delay is not randomized, so this is not an isolated causal forgetting estimate. Adjusting for delay does not reveal a coupling effect.

Experiment 3 reports no significant interface effect. First-run times are 18/21 minutes for AND/OR and 23/25 for indirect addressing (global/parameter); total think times are 26/29 and 44/43. Mean runs are 2.4/2.4 and 4.0/4.7. Comprehension means are 10.6/11.4. Exact model results and uncertainty bounds for this experiment are not provided. The two rounded task means sum to 70 versus 72 minutes, a description of this protocol rather than a precise estimate of equal net maintenance cost.

The paper describes statistical entries as statistic/significance-level pairs, sometimes coarse levels. They are retained as printed rather than relabeled as independently verified exact p-values. There are no equivalence tests, raw variances or power bounds supporting absence of a meaningful effect. Repeated tasks, small cells, attrition, several outcomes/comparisons and one program constrain inference. The strongest supported reading is **no consistent interface effect across these experiments**, alongside a stable task-difficulty difference and failure of the initial favorable timing comparison to recur.

Correctness is a stopping condition against supplied expected output, not an independently reported all-obligation endpoint. Test coverage, hidden regressions, incomplete attempts under the two-hour limit and later repair costs are not reconstructed. The number of runs is only a process proxy; it is not the number or severity of behavioral failures. Modifications can also differ in longer-term integration quality, which the authors explicitly acknowledge as unmeasured.

## Consequences and follow-up

**Unique:** controlled, same-algorithm source-convention comparisons and replication of a favorable subgroup result have longstanding precedents. **Valuable:** disagreement between static metrics and repeated task outcomes supports measuring complete behavior and cost directly; it does not establish that globals, parameters or Nu are generally preferable. **Scientifically valid:** freeze the source convention and feedback opportunity, retain attrition and all attempts, distinguish measurement from intervention, and represent both participant and task-family uncertainty. The three experiments do not validate D1's equivalence/oracle, a functional-language mechanism or an agent effect.

The industrial positive counterpoint in reference [12], Troy/Zweben's *Measuring the Quality of Structured Designs*, is verified at `10.1016/0164-1212(81)90031-5`. Its [publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0164121281900315) relates design measures to subsequent modification counts and reports explained variation, but the design, evaluation split and causal interpretation require its body.
