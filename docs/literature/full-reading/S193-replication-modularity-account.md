## Why this source changes the modularity synthesis

[S90](S90-modularity-program-modifiability.md) reports a large benefit for changing inventory access behind three module boundaries. Section 4.4 here reports the replication investigators' own different result and interpretation. That is primary author testimony about their study, though it lacks the original publication's complete design/data. It narrows an unqualified claim of reproducible fourfold benefit without establishing zero benefit.

The replicated task changes a Turbo Pascal inventory/point-of-sale program. The modular and inlined versions are described as approximately **1,000 and 1,400 lines**. This conflicts with S90's claim that length was held constant; exact sources and counting conventions remain unrecovered. S90's adaptive label and this chapter's perfective label are also different descriptions of the concrete task, not proof of distinct data.

The replication reports mean times **48 minutes modular versus 59.1 monolithic**, with no statistically significant difference. The means remain modular-favorable—approximately an 18.8% reduction—but are much closer than S90's 19.3 versus 85.9. The chapter gives no variance, interval or test statistic for this comparison; a meaningful positive effect is not excluded by the reported null. Only **nine monolithic participants** are explicitly counted here. Do not invent the modular count, exclusions or hidden-oracle policy from that number.

After observing the unexpected result, the authors build a database and perform an inductive analysis. All nine monolithic participants fall within the fastest twelve pretest ranks. They interpret random assignment as having placed more capable participants in that condition and recommend ability-based balancing in retrospect. This is a plausible **post hoc explanation**, not an independently identified ability effect or a demonstration that another allocation would restore the original result. S90 itself found unstable preliminary-task rankings, so neither account establishes a transportable ability score.

Observed/debriefed strategies also differ: four slow modular participants seek more detailed understanding; the two fastest monolithic finishers report little understanding of the code. The authors call the latter targeted editing strategy pragmatic maintenance and redirect attention to how maintainers actually work. Strategy is observed after assignment, and self-report is not a direct comprehension measurement. Conditioning on strategy would not automatically estimate the pure source-treatment effect. Fast, correct edits need not demonstrate broad comprehension and should not be rejected merely for taking advantage of the task structure.

## Replication framework and material reporting — pp. 365–379

The chapter distinguishes replication by original/overlapping investigators from replication by independent investigators, then describes similarities, alternatives and improvements across **method, task and subjects**. Its own replication is classified as improved method, similar task and similar subjects, because it adds debriefing. These dimensions help record actual differences; external authorship alone does not ensure comparable measurements or sufficient sensitivity.

General arguments, historical anecdotes, literature counts and methodological recommendations are not new controlled evaluations. Its strongest language about the confirming power of external replication and teaching experiments remains author argument. Secondary reports of flowchart, OO-development and other studies are not adopted as newly reconstructed empirical outcomes.

Section 6 identifies concrete information missing from the original account: whether monitors controlled phase transitions; more precise experience eligibility; timings for three available test procedures; original pretest times; and spoken instructions. These omissions affect interpretation of time and cross-study comparability. They do not show that all original observations were invalid. The authors explicitly credit Korson for publishing source, instructions, participant timings and tests in the dissertation, which is still unavailable to this reading.

The proposed remedy is a laboratory package containing code, task and verbal instructions, questionnaires, raw data, validity threats and the fuller report. This is a reproducibility/reporting recommendation, not evidence that a particular available package reproduces either study. No author programs, experiments or new analyses of unavailable individual replication data ran here.

**Disposition:** S90 supplies substantial selected-task timing benefits plus a fourth-task null. This external replication supplies a smaller favorable point estimate with inconclusive inference and hypotheses about ability/strategy differences. Preserve both rather than count significance outcomes as votes or promote post hoc strategy explanations into identified causal mechanisms. Source organization, permitted task strategies, observed effort and independently checked behavior are separate.
