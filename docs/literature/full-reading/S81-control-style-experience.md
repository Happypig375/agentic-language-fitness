## Design and measured construct

B01/B10/B11/B12 concern expertise-dependent architecture advice and the difference between faster successful submissions and better outcomes across all assignments. This is direct maintenance evidence within Java, not a functional-versus-object-oriented language experiment. It is also the D4/P9 source previously used at abstract/selected-passage scope in the [architecture review](../../architecture-paradigm-scope-review-2026-09-14.md).

The experiment assigned **158 people: 99 consultants and 59 students**, blocked by five developer categories and randomized between two coffee-machine implementations. Each person received one implementation. Company managers selected available consultants and classified/priced them as junior, intermediate or senior; the categories and expertise itself were not randomized. Graduate and undergraduate students volunteered. Professional sessions used normal offices and preferred development tools; students worked in a lab, generally using Emacs and Javac. Twelve sessions, different tool/education contexts, compensation and company selection constrain population and expertise comparisons.

The centralized-control (CC) design has seven classes, with `FrontPanel` controlling product selection, prices and preparation. The delegated-control (DC) design has twelve classes, passing responsibilities through products, recipes, ingredients and registers. Both derive from Cockburn's coffee-machine example, with common coding/naming/comment conventions and supplied UML sequence diagrams. More than one structural feature changes; there is no formal operational definition of control style (§6.1), and these small examples do not span all architectures. Independent starting-behavior equivalence was not verified in this reading.

A training task reverses input numbers; a separate bank-teller pretest supplies a skill proxy. Four coffee changes then occur in **one fixed sequence**, carrying each person's earlier solution forward: coin-return button, bouillon product, ingredient availability and customized drinks. The final task deliberately maintains time pressure and is **excluded from the reported outcome analysis**. The analyzed three-task chain is not three independent applications or a sampled task family. The session limit was eight hours; professionals initially received a fixed five-hour fee, with additional payment offered to finish afterward, while student compensation was fixed. These rules and pilots were intended to let almost everyone finish the first three tasks.

Effort combines self-recorded minutes on c1–c3, checked against the SESE timestamps, excluding gaps between tasks. Participants received test cases and expected outputs and could use their tools. Later, an independent consultant uninformed of the hypotheses compiled and tested submissions, inspected source differences and manually judged logic. He reviewed the grading twice, taking about 200 hours for almost 1,000 programs including training/pretest submissions. Correct means all three changes have no serious logical errors and no output difference beyond cosmetic variation. Design blinding and complete oracle sensitivity are not established. The supplied expected outputs were already visible to participants; this was not a wholly hidden behavioral test suite.

The two incomplete participants, one CC senior and one DC intermediate, are absent from the timing model and remain unsuccessful in the assigned correctness denominator. Completion is not correctness. The rounded overall means are both 91 minutes; this does not establish exact or practical equivalence. Complete-chain correctness is 54/78 = 69.23% for CC and 40/80 = 50% for DC, a descriptive 19.23 percentage-point difference. The unadjusted DC-versus-CC odds ratio is .444; it is distinct from the adjusted model below, and an odds ratio is not a risk ratio.

Senior timing means are 103 versus 71 minutes. DC takes about 31.1% less time using CC as denominator; CC takes about 45.1% more using DC. The p. 529 wording that CC takes about 30% more uses an inconsistent percentage denominator. This arithmetic issue does not erase the observed direction or validate a general experience threshold.

## What the inferential analyses support

**All timing-complete participants:** Table 5 fits log effort for 156 people, with design, developer category and their interaction. The design term has p = .964, developer category p = .001, and the planned five-category interaction **p = .133**. Neither a supported interaction at .05 nor an equivalence test is obtained. Post hoc Tukey comparisons distinguish graduates from juniors/intermediates, not every experience pair.

The authors then combine seniors/intermediates as high experience and students/juniors as low experience. The interaction has **p = .028**, but the paper explicitly says the grouping was chosen from the observed data and is exploratory. The abstract/conclusion's expertise recommendation must therefore retain that qualification. The descriptive crossover is a hypothesis about conditional effects, not confirmation of a prespecified rule for selecting designs by expertise.

**Correctness across all assignments:** Table 6's logistic model reports adjusted DC-versus-CC odds ratio **.40, 95% CI .20–.79, p = .009**. The interaction was removed as nonsignificant and harmful to fit; its exact omnibus p-value is not supplied. The common design coefficient is evidence for lower DC success within this model. Absence of a detected interaction does not prove equal effects across categories or establish each subgroup's separate benefit. Model-fit checks do not independently establish causal or sampling assumptions.

**Timing conditioned on correct submissions:** Table 7 restricts analysis to **94 successes**, adjusts for log pretest time and omits category because of its relationship to the pretest. DC's design term has p = .003. This is a post-assignment selection: the assigned design itself changes success probability. Adjusting for the observed pretest does not restore the randomized comparison among the selected successes or include the effort/cost of failures. It is not an all-attempt net-maintenance advantage and should not supersede the lower DC correctness rate. The coefficient's coding is not used here to invent an unreported percentage speedup.

## Limits and consequences

The study offers stronger evidence than source metrics or predicted maintenance, but 158 people do not supply 158 independent application/task families. The coffee program, fixed change order, short exposure, compound design differences and unvalidated oracle scope remain central. Long-run structural stability is discussed, not measured as a lifetime maintenance benefit. The paper describes eight companies in its opening and nine in the execution/acknowledgment material; that provenance discrepancy remains unresolved. Manager labels, education and tools cannot establish that experimentally acquiring expertise causes the observed differences, or that a coding agent is analogous to a human novice.

**Unique:** controlled source-structure comparisons and experience-sensitive maintenance hypotheses have direct prior art. The narrow Nu/F#/agent contrast remains unconfirmed, not rescued as novel by weaknesses in this experiment. **Valuable:** the conflict between selected-success speed and all-assignment correctness makes a complete behavioral endpoint useful in principle; no Nu prevalence, effect or net benefit is measured. **Scientifically valid:** retain every assignment, separate incompletion from failure, preserve task-chain dependence, predefine interactions and experience information, and validate common initial behavior and old/new obligations. These are design requirements, not authorization or validation of a new experiment.
