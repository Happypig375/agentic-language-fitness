## Mechanism and preparation cost

SyDRA combines a manually assigned subsystem map with an automatically extracted C++ **include graph**, then loads and visualizes the merged model in Moose. Its six steps are system selection, subsystem selection, manual file/folder classification, include extraction with `cinclude2dot`, model generation and visualization. Names, folder structure, documentation and source comments inform classification. The final two steps are semi-automatic. In/out degree and betweenness describe the resulting graph; includes are not dynamic calls, actual data flow or a complete semantic change-impact relation.

The authors selected ten popular GitHub C++ game engines by keyword/language/star count. They classified them using **sixteen subsystems from Gregory's 2018 reference architecture**. This gives a common vocabulary, but finding those categories in nine of ten engines is not independent discovery of a universal architecture: the categories were imposed before coding. The first author alone performed the manual mapping. The paper reports **about six months for the ten-engine analysis, including about two months of subsystem detection**. Those preparation costs are outside the user-study task times; no amortized net benefit is measured.

The graph examples are useful diagnostic hypotheses. Godot and Unreal have all sixteen categories; most other selected engines have at least twelve, while olcPixelGameEngine has five. Core and renderer dependencies occur frequently. Figure 9 arranges common links using frequency and centrality, rather than deriving an optimal architecture. Figure 3 additionally displays an `OTH` tag; exhaustive, exclusive assignment to the sixteen named categories was not independently checked. Platform/renderer dependencies can have a legitimate functional reason, illustrated by Panda3D's graphics-state guardian. O3DE's proposed folder reorganization is **an unimplemented recommendation**. Neither smaller coupling nor flatter folders has a measured downstream benefit in that example.

The paper explicitly leaves professional evaluation of its proposed refactorings/reference architecture to future work. Contemporary claims about an engine's physics library, performance or development costs should not be inferred from its dated examples. News reports motivating Frostbite/Creation Engine problems are secondary background, not new primary measurements in this study.

## What the human study assigns and observes

Sixteen participants were recruited by email or in person, with prior object-oriented programming experience. They were randomly allocated to **eight independent participants per group**, one session each, on **one system, Godot**. Control used VS Code; treatment used VS Code plus Moose, the recovered architectural map, subsystem tags and linked navigation. Treatment therefore receives prepared information and additional tools together. This does not isolate visual notation, classification, architecture quality or source organization.

Both groups received tool-specific illustrated instructions and three optional warm-ups. Seven understanding tasks ask for subsystem descriptions, features, implementing files and dependencies. Two impact tasks ask which files might change when removing rich text or adding video playback speed. **Participants identify locations; they do not implement or test changes.** Several statements prescribe different navigation steps by arm. Tasks are randomized except dependent Tasks 3 and 4. An observer provides clarification/technical assistance. The form measures intervals between submitted answers, without an independently reconstructed accounting of interruptions, navigation or warm-up time.

The structure and six outcome names come from [S155](S155-quality-guidelines-design-documents.md), but S155's actual comparison concerns different conforming/degraded design documents, not a same-program model-versus-source contrast. In SyDRA, understanding correctness is a binary score per task, summed to seven. Impact completeness is described as recall of required files; impact correctness is precision of named files; both task ratios are summed, nominally to two. Rate counts correct files per unit of impact time. These are neither behavioral correctness nor successful maintenance.

The package's demographics show eight students/researchers, five non-game developers, two game developers and one teacher; thirteen men and three women, mostly resident in Canada/Brazil. The experience question records seven people who never used an engine, whereas the multiple-choice engine list records eight such selections. Neither equals the paper's **57%**. The sixteen outcome rows establish eight per group; a different demographic denominator or correction is not documented in the inspected material. Participant IDs recur across groups and must not be treated as paired people.

## Reported benefits and adverse outcomes

The preprint's Table 6, repeated in the inspected journal extraction, reports:

| Outcome | Control / treatment mean | Printed effect size / “Z” | Authors' interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Understanding minutes | 65.5 / 49.2 | .4 / 20.0 | Faster with SyDRA |
| Correct understanding answers, max seven | 5.9 / 6.2 | .3 / 10.5 | Listed as significant, but narrative also says correctness is unaffected |
| Impact-analysis minutes | 21.4 / 32.0 | 1.7 / 5.0 | Longer with SyDRA; no significant difference claimed |
| Impact completeness, nominal max two | 1.0 / 2.0 | 1.5 / 5.0 | No significant difference claimed |
| Impact precision, nominal max two | 1.1 / 1.5 | .5 / 7.0 | Higher with SyDRA |
| Correct files per time | 0.0 / 0.0 | .2 / 12.0 | Claimed significant despite coarse printed rounding |

The abstract's general wording about less time and greater correctness should not replace these outcome-specific directions. More time on impact analysis could accompany more thorough searching; the authors' explanation of premature stopping in control is an interpretation, not a separately randomized stopping-policy effect. NASA-TLX-derived five-point responses favor treatment on mental demand, effort and frustration, with **higher physical demand** and similar temporal demand/perceived success. These are immediate reported workload dimensions, not evidence of lower total labor or no burden.

## Reconstruction and inferential limits

The following discrepancies are concrete, not a claim that the method has no benefit:

1. **Independent groups, paired analysis.** Section 5.7 says Wilcoxon matched pairs/signed ranks, although each person receives only one treatment. No justified matching scheme is reported. Recurring local form IDs do not provide one. It calls values such as 20.0 “Z” and declares values exceeding five significant. The package's `E_significance_results.csv` additionally labels **8.438, 6.049, 781, 781, 2.719 and 7.768** as p-values. As stored they are not probabilities. Missing decimal/exponent information, a different intended statistic and the original analysis cannot be reconstructed by guessing. No corrected p-values or reanalysis are claimed.
2. **Tables are mutually inconsistent.** With eight people per group, the printed understanding means imply a pooled mean of **57.35 minutes**, not Table 5's 62.0; the impact means imply **26.7**, not 31.4. These differences exceed rounding. Table 5 also swaps the descriptive labels of completeness and correctness relative to the definitions.
3. **Released rows are a third account.** Direct averages of the sixteen released rows give understanding **72.963 / 59.086 minutes** and impact **21.379 / 41.368 minutes**. Task-time sums agree with each row's stored phase totals. Pooled understanding is **66.025 minutes**, with a maximum 183.695, rather than the paper/package summary's 62.0 and 128.7. Pooled impact, **31.373 minutes**, agrees with Table 5 after rounding. The package's group-summary seconds agree with these raw means; the PDF's converted group means do not. An undocumented cleaning/version difference remains possible, but is not established.
4. **Completeness is not a validated recall denominator.** Four released scores exceed the package's stated maximum two, up to 3.61. All sixteen scores match, to rounding, `Q8_correct/11 + Q9_correct/3`; this is a local inferred formula, not a verified answer key. Some “correct” counts exceed those inferred required totals. Multiple acceptable solutions require an explicit scoring rule before these can be interpreted as comparable recall.
5. **Empty-answer precision handling is unresolved.** Control row 16 has seven correct of twelve files for Task 8 and no files for Task 9, yet overall precision zero. Treatment row 13 has no Task 8 files and three correct of three for Task 9, also overall zero. Summing the defined task contributions would give approximately .58 and 1.0. No inspected rule explains suppressing the other task when one denominator is empty. This does not authorize silently changing their recorded outcome.
6. **Planning does not guarantee significance.** The power-planning file uses prior-study deviations and deltas of 60 for times; consistency with the prior study's units and the stated accuracy threshold is not established. Exceeding a planned participant count cannot ensure a significant result. No multiple-outcome adjustment is described. A nondetected time difference also does not establish equivalent time, nor does a small estimated effect itself settle external validity.

The released outcomes preserve the **descriptive direction** of lower understanding time, higher recorded understanding/precision scores and greater impact-analysis time. They do not reconcile the precise effect estimates, recall interpretation or significance claims. A corrected analysis would need documented units, scoring keys, cleaning rules, assignment and independent-group inference; that work is not represented as completed here.

The package also states that exact source-browser propagation depended on workarounds in the authors' Moose image, with a suggested VS Code workaround for others. That is a **dated package reproducibility limit**, not a verified claim about current Moose. The repository README was inspected, but its API commit lookup returned 403; no pinned analyzer source or full model recovery was reconstructed. No installations or source execution occurred.

## Consequences and further leads

**Unique:** unconfirmed. Recovering subsystem structure, displaying dependencies and supporting human impact analysis are established alternatives to changing an implementation's language or architecture. SyDRA's 2023 predecessor, `10.1007/978-981-99-8248-6_22`, contributes the same method/data lineage; it is not independent validation. Other recovered routes include Quake architecture analysis, AC2/Unreal evolution, static runtime-architecture recovery and textual/graphical documentation comparisons. Their full methods remain conditional.

**Valuable:** the concrete recovery/navigation mechanism and favorable descriptive understanding result are relevant to existing-engine work. Manual model preparation, tool adaptation, adverse impact time and absent implementation/lifetime measures prevent a net-cost or Nu-transfer conclusion. **Scientifically valid:** the selected paper, tasks, released summaries and bounded raw outcomes are reconstructed; inferential and scoring inconsistencies remain unresolved. Criticizing these does not validate ISE.

G25's seven incoming citation edges and SC90–91's exact lookups add specific follow-up rather than field completeness. **S159** is selected for industrial game-engine instrumentation, coverage cost and test stability. The 2025 fuzzy-metric game-engine maintainability paper and 2026 build-accessibility study remain method-level leads; static scores must not be equated with observed maintenance. The 2D ECS/OOP scalability preprint was already a conditional W01 lead; it is not newly discovered independent evidence. The graph context that assigns an architecture-recovery-method citation to *What makes a game high-rated?* requires primary reference verification before using that linkage.
